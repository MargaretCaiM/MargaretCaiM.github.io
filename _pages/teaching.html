A family friend once told me the two hardest things in the world: the first is to draw money out of someone else's pocket and into your own; the second, and by far the harder, is to plant an idea from your own mind into someone else's and have it take root. Marketing is, at its core, the discipline of doing both — and teaching is the work of showing others how. That conviction is the root of how I teach.

I see education not simply as the transfer of knowledge, but as the work of empowering students to learn how to learn. My classroom runs on doing rather than listening: student teams sit in the decision maker's seat, argue a course of action out through case studies, data analysis, and AI tools, and end the term pitching a full marketing plan in a live roadshow. The central job of a marketing education, I believe, is to train judgment — knowing which problem is worth framing, which question is worth asking, and whether an answer actually holds up.

At UNC, I was the sole instructor for Principles of Marketing, earning a median teaching evaluation of 5.0/5.0, served as the teaching assistant for the MBA course on Retail and E-tail Marketing, and supported EMBA programs at UNC and at Washington University in St. Louis. Having moved from China to the United States, I know what it is like to be the newcomer in the room, and I draw on examples from both Asian and Western markets to help a diverse cohort think globally.
